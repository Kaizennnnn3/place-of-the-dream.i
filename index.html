01 / JAPAN

Mount Fuji

Shizuoka & Yamanashi Prefectures

Rising 3,776 meters in a near-perfect volcanic cone, Fuji has been Japan's most sacred natural monument since the 7th century, when pilgrims first climbed its slopes seeking spiritual purification.

Still an active stratovolcano, its symmetry has inspired centuries of art — from Hokusai's woodblock prints to the cherry-blossom-framed views that draw photographers to the Fuji Five Lakes each spring.

02 / SWITZERLAND

The Swiss Alps

Airolo, Ticino Canton

Emerald meadows roll up into jagged, snow-dusted peaks across the Swiss Alps — a range that has shaped European identity for centuries as a crossroads of trade routes, mountaineering, and alpine farming culture.

Above Airolo, cloud shadows drift across high pastures where cattle graze within sight of glaciers, and every valley reveals a different postcard view.

03 / NORWAY

Lofoten Fjords

Reinebringen, Lofoten Islands

Sheer granite peaks plunge straight into Arctic waters across the Lofoten archipelago, where fishing villages cling to narrow strips of shoreline beneath mountains carved by ancient glaciers.

The iconic view from Reinebringen — a jagged ridge overlooking Reine's red cabins and turquoise water — has become one of the most photographed vistas in Scandinavia.

04 / ICELAND

Seljalandsfoss

South Coast, Iceland

Fed by meltwater from the Eyjafjallajökull glacier, Seljalandsfoss plunges 60 meters over a cliff that once formed part of Iceland's coastline — a walking path even circles behind the curtain of falling water.

Iceland's south coast is a procession of waterfalls, black-sand beaches, and volcanic fields, all shaped by the restless geology sitting atop the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.

05 / NEW ZEALAND

Milford Sound

Fiordland National Park, South Island

Rudyard Kipling once called it the eighth wonder of the world — a fjord carved by glaciers into sheer rock walls that rise straight from the sea, laced with dozens of waterfalls that appear whenever it rains.

Part of Te Wahipounamu, a UNESCO World Heritage site, Milford Sound sits within one of the wettest and most pristine wilderness regions left on Earth.
